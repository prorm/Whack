# Controlled pilot protocol

## Question and participants

Can a novice handle unfamiliar stock figures after watching a contributor identify and fix a real error? Separately, does source capture save an expert time compared with documenting the same work from scratch? Recruit a feasibility cohort of 24–36 consenting adults from a vocational program or two small-business partners, plus at least two inventory educators or trainers. Record organization and prior spreadsheet experience as strata. Keep hiring, grading and work assignments independent of participation. The cohort can uncover workflow problems and effect-size uncertainty. It is too small for strong comparative claims.

## Design

Ask for a short baseline task and randomly assign within prior-experience strata.

1. **Generic worked example.** Written steps with the same arithmetic, examples, practice duration and transferable terms.
2. **Expert-derived walkthrough.** Source replay and contributor rationale with the same exercises and time budget.
3. **Expert walkthrough plus repair.** Same source and practice with a previously scheduled misconception-based retrieval prompt.

Use the training item for illustration only. Assign balanced, unseen worksheets from the independently authored keys for immediate performance and after a fixed seven-day delay. Use an additional independent variation in which the physical count is higher than the ledger. Require both amounts and optional confidence (0–100). Score blind to group if possible. Track correct expected values, correct sign, four-point grading rubric for two items, completion and time. Separately capture reasons for changing course, audio refusal, device difficulties, and whether the footage helped explain the decision.

Recruit contributors to complete the same workflow with and without a draft, in balanced order with different problems. Track active minutes, count of original steps, corrections, uncertainty flags and changed explanations. A second reviewer checks citations and independently verifies calculations while unaware of the draft route. Preserve rejected steps and moderator rationale.

## Learning mechanisms and risks

| Mechanism | Implementation | Expected measure or limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Retrieval and spacing | Recall before showing the equation and schedule a return using the existing event engine | Compare delayed signed variance and completion. Prompt availability and engagement confound interpretable data |
| Worked examples and scaffolding | Source trace, same-row highlight and optional visual calculation | Compare baseline-adjusted move to an unseen worksheet. Avoid overloading users with video and text simultaneously |
| Fade support and vary context | Remove the expected total in the practical challenge and vary shortages, surpluses and zero | Accuracy in altered contexts. More variants need independent keys |
| Feedback and error correction | Show which signed calculation failed and the actual corrected equation after a response | Record wrong-to-right transitions and avoid giving answer exposure before delayed recall |
| Interleaving and teach-back | Later rotate task types and invite a short private explanation | Clear explanations may reflect language fluency. A human rubric is needed |
| Authenticity | Use the contributor's cited row and rationale | Compare real task comprehension, with no employment-readiness inference |

Retrieval and transfer literature motivates the design. The pilot does not assume learners have fixed format preferences. [Roediger and Karpicke experimental paper](https://psychnet.wustl.edu/memory/wp-content/uploads/2018/04/Roediger-Karpicke-2006_PsychSci.pdf) · [Butler experimental paper](https://andymatuschak.org/files/papers/Butler%20-%202010%20-%20Repeated%20Testing%20Produces%20Superior%20Transfer%20of%20Learning%20Relative%20to%20Repeated.pdf)

## Consent script for partner review

“Taking part is optional and has no effect on training or employment. We save your worksheet answers and task times for the study. You may separately choose whether to record a short explanation, whether to let the study reviewer hear it, and whether to let us use a redacted recording in training materials. You can stop or withdraw through the study coordinator. We will explain the retention window and any data that has already been de-identified before you decide.”

Use synthetic data for dry runs. Collect separate, revocable checkboxes before private recording, derivative learning use, publicity or employer sharing. Agree on minimal retention and data handling with partners. Avoid learner faces and real inventory/customer records. Report dropout, missing items and any deviations. Analyze immediate and delayed outcomes by assigned group, show descriptive estimates and uncertainty, and label any exploratory association. No human data has been collected.
